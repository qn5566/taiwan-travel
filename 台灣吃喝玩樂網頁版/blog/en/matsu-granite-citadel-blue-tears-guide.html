🌊 Maritime & Frontier Chronicle: The Mediterranean of the Taiwan Strait

Clinging to the jagged continental shelf off the Min River estuary, the 36 granite islands of Matsu (Nangan, Beigan, Dongyin, and Juguang) form Taiwan’s northernmost outpost. Named after the sea goddess Mazu whose physical body was said to have washed ashore on Nangan, these islands possess a cultural identity entirely distinct from mainland Taiwan: they are the only territory under Taiwanese administration that speaks the Eastern Fujianese (Mindong) Fuzhou dialect.

During the Cold War, Matsu was fortified into an impenetrable military iron curtain with the highest density of underground tunnels anywhere on Earth. Hand-chiseled into solid granite, these labyrinthine waterways accommodated fleets of armed gunboats. Contrasting this martial past are the poetic, amber-granite shophouses of Qinbi Village overlooking cobalt sea channels, and the natural miracle of the glowing nocturnal "Blue Tears" (藍眼淚).

🏰 Military Megastructures & Bioluminescent Seas

Landmark / Experience Historical Significance & Architecture Best Timing & Season Access & Booking
The Blue Tears (馬祖藍眼淚) Millions of bioluminescent ostracods and dinoflagellates (Noctiluca scintillans) illuminating crashing waves in electric neon-blue. April to June: Southerly sea winds and ocean temperatures around 20°C–23°C trigger peak blooms. Beihai Night Sampan Pass
Beihai Underground Tunnel (南竿北海坑道) A submerged subterranean canal carved through solid granite over 820 days by Taiwanese army engineers working in shifts. Walk the tidal perimeter boardwalk at low tide, or row wooden sampan boats into the cavern at night. Google Maps Navigation
Qinbi Stone Hamlet (芹壁聚落) Known as "The Mediterranean of Matsu." Tiered granite houses with fire-horse walls (風火山牆) and anti-piracy roof tiles held by stones. Late afternoon: sip siphon coffee on stone balconies while the sun sets behind Turtle Island rock. Google Maps Navigation
Dongyin Lighthouse (東引島燈塔) An 18-meter-tall pure white brick lighthouse constructed in 1904 by British engineers atop a sheer 97-meter-tall sea cliff. Sunrise: watch the earliest daylight hit Taiwan’s northernmost territory above endless Pacific ocean swells. Google Maps Navigation
