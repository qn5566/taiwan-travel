🌊 Field Report: The 36 Granite Stepping Stones of the Northern Frontier

Clinging to the jagged continental shelf off the Min River estuary, the 36 wave-battered islands of Matsu (Nangan, Beigan, Dongyin, and Juguang) form Taiwan’s most dramatic maritime frontier. Named after the sea goddess Mazu whose physical body was said to have washed ashore on Nangan, these islands possess an identity completely distinct from mainland Taiwan: they are the only territory under Taiwanese administration that speaks the Eastern Fujianese (Mindong) Fuzhou dialect.

Here, between April and June, millions of bioluminescent dinoflagellates (Noctiluca scintillans) bloom in the coastal upwelling currents, illuminating crashing breakers with an otherworldly electric-cyan glow known globally as the Blue Tears (藍眼淚). Yet for international explorers, the blue light is merely the gateway: Matsu is a subalpine-style granite playground featuring sheer 100-meter cliffs rising straight out of the roaring surf, underground naval canals hand-chiseled through solid rock, and the stark remnants of Cold War border fortresses.

🎖️ The Conscript Chronicles: From "Golden Horse" Ghost Lore to Tranquil Island Breezes

For decades across Taiwan, whenever older generations gather to drink high-mountain tea, conversations inevitably drift toward their mandatory military service on the offshore islands. Drawing deployment to Kinmen or Matsu was colloquially termed winning the "Golden Horse Award" (金馬獎)—a tongue-in-cheek euphemism for the dreaded lottery that sent young recruits across rough sea channels, leaving tearful families behind at Keelung Harbor.

Isolated from civilization under total blackout conditions, the islands birthed countless barracks ghost stories: phantom footsteps echoing in abandoned tunnels during dense spring fogs, mysterious midnight sobbing carried over the water during sea-watch duty, sealed assembly halls, and decaying camouflage pillboxes overgrown with vines. In local folklore, Matsu took on the eerie reputation of a haunted fortress, shrouded in the quiet dread of an ancient mountain crypt.

Yet standing upon Matsu’s shores today, you realize that yesterday's dread has transformed into modern tranquility.

The mythical "ghostly weeping" was in fact the winter monsoon gale howling through narrow granite sea arches and wave-cut caves. The pitch-black tunnels that terrified young sentries were monumental feats of survival, carved inch by inch out of solid granite bedrock with pickaxes and dynamite. Today, anti-landing steel rails (軌條砦) embedded along sandy coves stand as rusted sculptural monuments against blazing Pacific sunsets; abandoned artillery ramparts bloom with wild sea lilies; and military broadcasting speakers that once beamed propaganda now echo with gentle oceanic silence.

Visitors encountering rusted bunkers, tunnel gun-ports, or crumbling barracks need feel no fear. These ghost stories and battle relics are neither curses nor omens; they are the honorable scars of history. Every subterranean canal and coastal observation post stands as a solemn testament to the youth of generations of island defenders, marking the island's journey from Cold War tension into profound, enduring peace.

🥾 Coastal Ridges & Fortress Trails: The Hiking Frontiers

High Ridge ｜ Sheer Ocean Cliffs

Mount Luobi & Luoshan Trail (螺山自然步道)

📍 Beigan Island, Northern Ridge Terminal

A knife-edge trail spanning a jagged granite spine that plunges into deep waters. Equipped with safety cables along steep drops, this 4.2 km trek traverses sea-carved arches, barren stone fields, and wind-stunted wild shrubs with panoramic views toward the Chinese mainland.

Dongyin Lighthouse & Thread of Sky (東引燈塔)

📍 Dongyin Island, Easternmost Sea-Bluff

Constructed by British architects in 1904, this pure white brick lighthouse commands a vertical 97-meter-tall cliff[cite: 1]. Follow the steep stone steps down to "A Thread of Sky" (一線天), where a narrow suspension bridge connects two towering rock chasms roaring with Atlantic swells.

Jiguang Biscuits (繼光餅 - The Matsu Burger)

📍 Baolingshi Bakery, Nangan

Created in 1563 by General Qi Jiguang to provision troops hunting maritime pirates, these sesame-studded sourdough breads are baked in clay ovens[cite: 1]. Sliced open and stuffed with sizzling minced pork and oyster-fried eggs, they serve as the island’s signature burger[cite: 1].
