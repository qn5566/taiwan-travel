🌊 Historical Chronicle: The Island of 90 Volcanic Stepping Stones

Scattered across the turquoise waters of the Taiwan Strait, the 90 islands of the Penghu Archipelago (the Pescadores) formed 18 million years ago from intraplate volcanic eruptions, cooling into sheer polygonal basalt columns. Centuries before Han settlements took root on mainland Taiwan, Penghu served as a critical nautical refuge for Song Dynasty seafarers, Dutch East India merchant galleons, Ming-Qing naval armadas, and French admirals contesting the 1884 Sino-French War.

Today, Penghu remains one of the planet's great natural and cultural crossroads. During the summer, calm tropical breezes reveal underwater lavender coral forests, sea turtle nesting grounds, and centuries-old stone tidal weirs (石滬). In autumn and winter, the ferocious Northeast Monsoon transforms the shallow bays into the world’s most celebrated windsurfing and kitesurfing battlegrounds.
