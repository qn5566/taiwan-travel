## Itinerary Positioning

This itinerary suits first-time visitors who want to combine old-town history, local street food, coffee culture and riverside scenery. The route follows a simple rhythm: one old district in the morning, one neighborhood in the afternoon, and a night market or riverside walk in the evening, avoiding too many cross-district transfers in a single day.

Day 1 explores Dadaocheng and the Tamsui River waterfront to understand Taipei’s earliest commercial district. Day 2 walks through Zhongshan, Shuanglian and Chifeng Street lane cafes, followed by Raohe Street Night Market in the evening. Day 3 keeps the pace light with a riverside walk, coffee, souvenir shopping and flexible departure-time adjustments.

### 1. [Dihua Street Section 1](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%E8%BF%AA%E5%8C%96%E8%A1%97%E4%B8%80%E6%AE%B5%20%E5%A4%A7%E7%A8%BB%E5%9F%95%20%E5%8F%B0%E5%8C%97%E5%B8%82): Taipei’s historic wholesale goods street

**Suggested stop:** 60–90 minutes  
**Transport:** About a 5-minute walk from MRT Beimen Station into the district. You can also start from Daqiaotou Station and walk from the northern end toward the southern end.  
**What to see:** Mixed Chinese and Western-style old shop houses, arcades, groceries, Chinese herbs, tea houses, fabric shops and newer creative boutiques.

Dihua Street is the core commercial street of Dadaocheng. During the boom of Tamsui River trade, groceries, tea, fabric and medicinal herbs were distributed through this area. While walking, look beyond the front facades: notice the arcade entrances, red brick and Western-style ornamentation, then duck into side alleys to see the still-functioning traditional commercial atmosphere.

**Old shops worth visiting:**

- [Ziyang Wagashi (Ziyang Doushipu)](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%E6%BB%8B%E9%A4%8A%E5%92%8C%E8%8F%93%E5%AD%90%20%E8%BF%AA%E5%8C%96%E8%A1%97%E4%B8%80%E6%AE%B5247%E8%99%9F): A Japanese-style confectionery shop with more than 70 years of history. Common items include daifuku, monaka, castella-like treats and seasonal strawberry daifuku. These sweets work well as an afternoon dessert or souvenir.
- [Li Ting Xiang Bakery](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%E6%9D%8E%E4%BA%AD%E9%A6%99%E9%A4%85%E5%BA%97%20%E8%BF%AA%E5%8C%96%E8%A1%97%E4%B8%80%E6%AE%B5309%E8%99%9F): A traditional Chinese pastry shop founded in 1895, with tea, coffee and baked goods inside. It is a good place to sit down after walking the old street.
- [You Ji Tea](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%E6%9C%89%E8%A8%98%E5%90%8D%E8%8C%B6%20%E5%A4%A7%E7%A8%BB%E5%9F%95): Suitable for visitors who want to learn about Taiwanese tea or purchase tea leaves. Ask about flavor profile and storage before buying.
- [Sin Fang Chun Tea House](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%E6%96%B0%E8%8A%B3%E6%98%A5%E8%8C%B6%E8%A1%8C%20%E5%A4%A7%E7%A8%BB%E5%9F%95): A historic tea house building that helps explain Dadaocheng’s tea-trading history. Check on-site notices for opening hours and exhibitions.

### 2. [Xiahai City God Temple](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%E5%8F%B0%E5%8C%97%E9%9C%9E%E6%B5%B7%E5%9F%8E%E9%9A%8D%E5%BB%9F): Dadaocheng’s spiritual center

**Suggested stop:** 20–30 minutes  
**Location:** No. 61, Section 1, Dihua Street. Verify the official address and opening hours before publication.  
**Highlights:** Temple history, architectural ornamentation, the Matchmaker deity tradition and surrounding old street scenery.

Xiahai City God Temple is an important historic and religious landmark in Dadaocheng. It has stood for more than 160 years and is also known for its Matchmaker deity tradition. The temple itself is not large, but it is densely filled with deities and often shared by worshippers and visitors. Keep quiet, follow the temple’s circulation rules, and avoid disturbing people who are praying.

### 3. [Yongle Market](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%E6%B0%B8%E6%A8%82%E5%B8%82%E5%A0%B4%20%E5%A4%A7%E7%A8%BB%E5%9F%95%20%E5%8F%B0%E5%8C%97) and Minle Street: lunch and traditional snacks

**Suggested stop:** 70–90 minutes  
**Food direction:** Rice-noodle cakes, rice noodle soup, plain sliced noodles, chicken rolls, fish-ball soup, braised dishes, side plates and traditional desserts.

Yongle Market is not only a wholesale and retail fabric district; the surrounding Minle Street, Anxi Street and Yanping North Road area still preserve many traditional snack shops. On your first visit, choose one staple dish, then add a side plate or soup rather than buying from every stall right away.

### 4. [Dadaocheng Visitor Center](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%E5%A4%A7%E7%A8%BB%E5%9F%95%E9%81%8A%E5%AE%A2%E4%B8%AD%E5%BF%83): district history and visitor information

**Suggested stop:** 30 minutes  
**Highlights:** District history displays, old shop scenes, costume-experience information and visitor assistance.

If you want to understand how Dadaocheng grew from riverfront trade into one of Taipei’s important commercial streets, start with the visitor center’s brief introduction. You can also check current events, markets and guided-tour information there. Whether costume experiences are open and whether reservations are required should follow on-site announcements.

### 5. [Dadaocheng Wharf](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%E5%A4%A7%E7%A8%BB%E5%9F%95%E7%A2%BC%E9%A0%AD%20%E5%8F%B0%E5%8C%97) and riverside trail: sunset by the water

**Suggested stop:** 45–60 minutes  
**Transport:** Walk from Dihua Street toward Huanhe North Road and the Tamsui River waterfront.  
**Highlights:** River views, wharf space, riverside trail, sunset and weekend markets or events.

Dadaocheng was closely tied to Tamsui River shipping; the wharf area is now mainly used for leisure walks. Late afternoon light by the river is softer and good for walking, photos and resting. River wind can be strong in autumn and winter, so bring a light jacket. In rain or strong wind, shorten the riverside part and rest at a nearby cafe or tea house.

**Dinner suggestion for Day 1:** Return to Zhongshan, Shuanglian or Taipei Main Station for beef noodle soup, braised pork rice, a Taiwanese diner or a cafe. Save the night market for Day 2 evening if you still have energy.

### 1. Zhongshan and Shuanglian: start with a Taipei breakfast

**Suggested stop:** 60 minutes  
**Transport:** MRT Zhongshan Station or Shuanglian Station.

### 3. Lunch: choose between a district restaurant or local noodles

**Option A: Local food around Zhongshan / Shuanglian**  
Good if you want to keep walking the lanes. Choose beef noodle soup, braised pork rice, dumplings, a Taiwanese diner or simple noodles.

**Option B: Xinyi / East District commercial area**  
Good if you want department stores, modern street scenery and chain restaurants. Transport takes longer; if you stayed up late the previous night, do not pack too many stops.

### 4. [Raohe Street Night Market](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%E9%A5%92%E6%B2%B3%E8%A1%97%E8%A7%80%E5%85%89%E5%A4%9C%E5%B8%82): a first-time Taipei night market

**Transport:** A few minutes’ walk from Exit 5, MRT Songshan Station. You can also walk from Houshanpi Station on the Blue Line.

- Queue in order; do not cut in.

- Keep your bag in front of you and do not leave valuables exposed in crowded areas.

### 5. [Rainbow Bridge](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%E5%BD%A9%E8%99%B9%E6%A9%8B%20%E5%9F%BA%E9%9A%86%E6%B2%B3%20%E6%9D%BE%E5%B1%B1) and Keelung Riverfront: a short walk after the market

**Suggested stop:** 20–30 minutes  
**Highlights:** Riverfront night views, bridge lighting and a post-dinner digestive walk.

From the market to the Keelung River, Rainbow Bridge is a recognizable red S-shaped bridge at night. If you still have energy, take a short riverside walk and look at the bridge lights and night scenery. Do not plan a far-flung evening stop afterward, to avoid missing the last train or becoming overtired. In rain, strong wind or slippery conditions, return to your accommodation directly.

## Day 3: Riverside Walk, Coffee, Souvenirs and Departure Prep

### Route Order

Breakfast near accommodation → Riverside trail or city park → Cafe or dessert shop → Souvenir shopping → Return to accommodation for luggage → Airport or station.

### 1. Riverside trail: finish the Taipei trip on foot

**Suggested stop:** 60 minutes  
**Suitable spots:** [Dadaocheng Riverside](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%E5%A4%A7%E7%A8%BB%E5%9F%95%E6%B2%B3%E6%BB%A8%E6%AD%A5%E9%81%93), [Shezi Island](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%E7%A4%BE%E5%AD%90%E5%B3%B6%20%E5%8F%B0%E5%8C%97), [Guandu](https://www.google.com/maps/search/?api=1&query=%E9%97%9C%E6%B8%A1%20%E5%8F%B0%E5%8C%97) or a nearby riverside park; choose by accommodation and weather.  
**Highlights:** River views, bike lane, city skyline, wetland or park green space.

Do not pack too many sights on Day 3. Pick a riverside trail near your accommodation, or ride a short YouBike segment. If the weather is hot or rainy, switch to an indoor cafe, a park pavilion or a department-store rest area.

### 2. Final coffee and dessert
