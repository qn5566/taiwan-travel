🏛️ Historical Roots: Taiwan's Sacred Forest & Indigenous Tsou Roots
Formed millions of years ago by intense tectonic collisions between the Philippine Sea and Eurasian plates, Alishan is home to 2,000-year-old giant red cypress trees long revered as divine guardians by the indigenous Tsou people. The Alishan Forest Railway, engineered in 1912 as one of only three mountain-climbing zig-zag railways left on Earth, transported ancient timber across steep cloud forests and today carries travelers into mystical alpine canopies over 2,200 meters above sea level.
✍️ Traveler's Journal: Across Time, Mist, and Mountain Soul

I have journeyed to Alishan countless times across different chapters of my life—first as a solo wanderer seeking a fresh start at dawn, and now as a parent introducing my children to the wonders of the deep woods.

The winding mountain roads can be unpredictable and challenging to navigate, making private car charters the most relaxing way to take in the sheer majesty of the shifting cloud forests. The weather in these highlands changes in the blink of an eye: I remember a freezing February evening where the skies suddenly cleared into a crystalline tapestry of stars; yet by morning, a gentle drizzle descended upon the slopes.

Walking through the giant tree trails wrapped in raincoats, Alishan took on the mythical atmosphere of a Studio Ghibli forest—filled with pure birdsong and the scent of damp moss. The crisp mountain air, the peaceful bans on private vehicles inside the reserve, and the fragrant red cypress architecture around Fenchihu wash away all urban exhaustion. It remains my go-to alpine escape year after year.

Alishan Traveler Essentials: Practical Field Advice

1. Mandatory Layered Clothing

While the Chiayi plains sit at 28°C (82°F), Alishan at 2,200m drops to 5°C–12°C (41°F–54°F). Bring a windproof waterproof outer shell, a fleece mid-layer, and a warm beanie for dawn.

2. Forest Railway vs. Private Charters

Mainline Forest Railway tickets open exactly 14 days ahead and sell out within seconds. Booking a private Klook charter van guarantees flexible luggage handling, tea stops, and zero road fatigue.

3. Avoid Mountain Sickness & Fog

Route 18 experiences thick alpine fog daily after 1:30 PM. Complete long drives in the morning. If sensitive to motion sickness, take motion medication before ascending the mountain curves.

4. Cash & Connectivity

While hotels accept credit cards, local tea growers, trail stalls, and night market vendors operate strictly on cash (TWD). An ATM is available at the 7-Eleven inside the Alishan Station plaza.
