Jiaoxi Waterslides, Wushi Harbor Surfing, Night Glamping & Milk Sea Adventures
🏛️ Historical Roots: The Emerald Lanyang Basin & Kavalan Agrarian Harmony
Sheltered behind the towering Snow Mountain Range, Yilan remained an untouched wetland sanctuary stewarded by the indigenous Kavalan tribe until early Qing pioneers cultivated its rich alluvial soils. Blessed with odorless sodium-bicarbonate thermal springs in Jiaoxi and bountiful catches from the Pacific Kuroshio current, Yilan embodies the quintessential Taiwanese spirit of tranquil agrarian slow living.
✍️ Traveler's Chronicle: Over 50 Visits to Taipei's Emerald Backyard

I have traveled to Yilan well over fifty times. What began as quick weekend escapes for hot springs has evolved into an enduring love affair with the land. Taiwan is a small island, but the difference across its compass points is breathtaking. Emerging from the Snow Mountain Tunnel, you are immediately greeted by the vast Pacific and the volcanic outline of Turtle Island (龜山島).

Here, the Central Mountain Range plunges directly toward the coast, creating microclimates of emerald paddies and thermal springs. In recent years, glamping overlooking the million-dollar night lights of the Lanyang Plain has become my favorite way to reconnect with nature. You can surf beginner-friendly beach breaks at Wushi Harbor in the morning, taste Michelin-caliber teppanyaki at noon, and soak in odorless, skin-smoothing sodium bicarbonate hot springs with waterslides in the evening. And through it all, the locals treat every international guest like long-lost family.
